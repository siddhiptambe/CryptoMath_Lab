EXPERIMENT

Extended Euclidean Algorithm

Interactive Cryptography and Number Theory Laboratory — explore the Extended Euclidean Algorithm, GCD and multiplicative inverses.

Aim

To implement the Extended Euclidean Algorithm to calculate the greatest common divisor (GCD) of two integers and determine the multiplicative inverse of an integer whenever it exists.

Objective

To apply Extended Euclidean Algorithm, determine the GCD of two integers, calculate Bézout coefficients x and y, identify whether a multiplicative inverse exists, and verify the results mathematically using Bézout's identity and modular arithmetic.

Cryptography Connection

The multiplicative inverse is required in algorithms such as RSA and the Affine Cipher when an inverse modulo m is needed.

What you will do: Enter a and b, generate the Euclidean steps, watch the coefficient table update, and verify the final GCD and inverse.
CONCEPTS

Relevant Theory

Understand the algorithm before running the simulation.

1. Euclidean Algorithm

For integers a and b, repeatedly divide:

a = bq + r

Replace (a, b) with (b, r) until the remainder becomes zero. The last non-zero remainder is gcd(a,b).

2. Extended Euclidean Algorithm

Calculates Bézout coefficients x and y satisfying:

ax + by = gcd(a,b)

Using Forouzan's forward recurrence, remainders and coefficients update concurrently without back-substitution.

3. Multiplicative Inverse

The inverse of a modulo m is a number x such that:

ax ≡ 1 (mod m)

The inverse exists only when gcd(a,m) = 1. If the EEA gives ax + my = 1, then x mod m is the inverse.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Find gcd(17, 43) and the inverse of 17 modulo 43

Step 1: Apply the Extended Euclidean Algorithm with inputs a = 17 and b = 43.

x = x4 = −5; y = y4 = 2

Step 2: Calculate the GCD and Bézout coefficients

The last non-zero remainder is r4 = 1, so gcd(17, 43) = 1. The corresponding Bézout coefficients are x = −5 and y = 2, satisfying 17(−5) + 43(2) = 1.

Step 3: Calculate and verify the multiplicative inverse

Inverse = x mod b = −5 mod 43 = 38
17 × 38 = 646 = 43 × 15 + 1
Therefore, 17 × 38 ≡ 1 (mod 43)

Verification is successful because dividing 646 by 43 leaves remainder 1. Hence, 38 is the multiplicative inverse of 17 modulo 43.

METHOD

Step-by-Step Procedure

Follow these steps during the virtual experiment.

  1. 1
    Choose inputs.

    Enter positive integers a and b. For multiplicative inverse, treat b as the modulus.

  2. 2
    Initialize base values.

    Set r−1 = a, x−1 = 1, y−1 = 0 and r0 = b, x0 = 0, y0 = 1.

  3. 3
    Calculate division & quotient.

    At each step i, compute quotient qi = ⌊ri−2 / ri−1⌋ and remainder ri = ri−2 mod ri−1, satisfying ri−2 = qiri−1 + ri.

  4. 4
    Update Bézout coefficients.

    Calculate xi = xi−2 − qixi−1 and yi = yi−2 − qiyi−1, maintaining ri = axi + byi.

  5. 5
    Identify the GCD.

    When rn+1 = 0, stop. The last non-zero remainder rn is gcd(a,b), with Bézout coefficients x = xn and y = yn.

  6. 6
    Check the inverse.

    If gcd(a,b) = 1, the multiplicative inverse of a modulo b is x mod b. If gcd(a,b) > 1, no inverse exists.

  7. 7
    Verify.

    Confirm Bézout's identity ax + by = gcd(a,b) and (a × inverse) mod b = 1.

Important: A multiplicative inverse does not exist when gcd(a,b) ≠ 1.
